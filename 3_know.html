Know Your Trash

Every item belongs to a waste type. Learn the types, see what happens when waste is thrown away the wrong way, and find out how to manage it at home and at school.

When waste is thrown away the wrong way

Waste in the wrong place does not disappear. It spreads and harms land, water, air and living things.

Land pollution

Trash dumped on roadsides, empty lots and overfilled dumpsites.

Problems it can cause

  • Clogged drains that lead to flooding
  • Bad smells and pests that spread disease
  • Harmful liquid from rotting trash soaking into the soil

Water pollution

Litter and waste washed into canals, rivers and lakes.

Problems it can cause

  • Unsafe water for drinking and washing
  • Fish and other water life get sick or die
  • Blocked waterways that cause floods

Air pollution

Smoke from burning trash and gases from rotting waste.

Problems it can cause

  • Smoke that irritates the eyes, nose and lungs
  • Burning plastic releases harmful chemicals
  • Rotting waste gives off gases that warm the planet

Ocean pollution

Plastic and trash that travels from the land into the sea.

Problems it can cause

  • Turtles, birds and fish swallow plastic or get tangled in it
  • Tiny plastic pieces enter the food chain
  • Dirty beaches and damaged reefs

How to manage waste

Small habits add up. Start with these where you live and study.

At home
  • Set up separate bins for biodegradable, recyclable, non-recyclable and hazardous waste.
  • Turn food scraps and dried leaves into compost.
  • Rinse and dry bottles and cans before you recycle them.
  • Bring a reusable bag and water bottle when you go out.
  • Keep old batteries and bulbs in a box until you can take them to a collection point.
  • Reuse jars, boxes and containers before you throw them away.
At school
  • Label classroom bins by color so everyone knows where waste goes.
  • Use both sides of the paper and reuse notebooks with empty pages.
  • Bring a refillable water bottle and a lunch box instead of single-use wrappers.
  • Start a class compost pit or garden for leaves and fruit peels.
  • Collect old electronics and batteries for a drop-off day.
  • Pass on old books and uniforms instead of throwing them away.
